\title{Who holds government debt?}

\author{Yiran Emma Ma}

\maketitle

\whenintegrated{\label{abstract}} % integrated doc: SST for labels
\begin{verbatimwrite}{Abstract.txt}
\noindent
Across advanced economies at similar debt-to-GDP ratios, the same stock of government debt is held by sharply different mixes of domestic non-banks, foreigners, and the banking sector.
I propose a cost-minimizing model in which a government places a given debt stock across three sectors with different marginal costs: non-banks with a country-specific capacity, foreigners with a common ceiling, and a banking system with no hard cap but convex placement costs.
The model predicts that countries with deeper non-bank investor bases absorb more debt at every level, that the bounded sectors saturate concavely, and that banking takes over the margin once total debt is high enough.
Using the Arslanalp--Tsuda panel of sovereign debt by holder for 32 advanced economies (1989--2021), a two-stage estimator finds a sharp kink in banking absorption at $102.7\%$ of GDP, with combined central- and private-bank marginal absorption rising by roughly $82$~percentage points above the threshold.
Non-bank concavity operates within country (capacity); foreign concavity operates across countries (common ceiling).
\end{verbatimwrite}
