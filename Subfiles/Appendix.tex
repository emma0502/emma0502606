\section{Proofs of model propositions}
\whenintegrated{\label{app:proofs}}

This appendix collects proofs of Propositions~\ref{prop:monotone},
\ref{prop:Sh}, and~\ref{prop:concave} stated in
Section~\ref{sec:model}. Throughout, $H_j\equiv\partial\text{MC}_j/\partial d_j$
denotes the slope of sector $j$'s marginal-cost schedule, with closed
forms given in equations~\eqref{eq:Hh}--\eqref{eq:Hb}; the structural
property used at every step is $H_j>0$ in each sector.

\paragraph{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:monotone}.}
Suppose, for contradiction, that $D$ rises but $d_h^\ast$ falls.
Because $H_h>0$ strictly, $\text{MC}_h$ falls, so the new equilibrium
shadow rate $\lambda$ is lower. The first-order condition
\eqref{eq:foc} then implies that $\text{MC}_f$ and $\Psi'(d_b)$ also
fall, so $d_f^\ast$ and $d_b^\ast$ fall as well (since $H_f,H_b>0$).
But then $d_h^\ast+d_f^\ast+d_b^\ast=D$ has declined, contradicting
the premise that $D$ rose. The same argument applies to $d_f^\ast$
and $d_b^\ast$ by symmetry. Strict positivity of every $H_j$
delivers strict inequality at any interior allocation. $\square$

\paragraph{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:Sh}.}
Differentiate the FOC \eqref{eq:foc} and the budget constraint
\eqref{eq:budget} with respect to $S_h$, holding $D$ fixed. Write
$K_h\equiv\partial\text{MC}_h/\partial S_h$ at fixed $d_h$. Then
\[
  H_h\,\mathrm{d}d_h + K_h\,\mathrm{d}S_h \;=\; \mathrm{d}\lambda,
  \quad
  H_f\,\mathrm{d}d_f \;=\; \mathrm{d}\lambda,
  \quad
  H_b\,\mathrm{d}d_b \;=\; \mathrm{d}\lambda,
  \quad
  \mathrm{d}d_h+\mathrm{d}d_f+\mathrm{d}d_b=0.
\]
Using the last three equations to eliminate $\mathrm{d}d_f,
\mathrm{d}d_b,\mathrm{d}\lambda$ in terms of $\mathrm{d}d_h$ yields
\[
  \Bigl(H_h+\frac{H_fH_b}{H_f+H_b}\Bigr)\,\mathrm{d}d_h
  \;=\;-K_h\,\mathrm{d}S_h.
\]
Because $\text{MC}_h$ depends on $d_h$ only through the share
$x\equiv d_h/S_h$, the chain rule gives
$K_h=-(\partial\text{MC}_h/\partial x)\,d_h/S_h^{2}=-H_h\,d_h/S_h<0$
whenever $d_h>0$. The bracket multiplying $\mathrm{d}d_h$ is strictly
positive, hence $\partial d_h^\ast/\partial S_h>0$. $\square$

\paragraph{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:concave}.}
Let $m\equiv\lambda-\bar r_h>0$. Differentiating the closed-form
solutions \eqref{eq:dhstar}--\eqref{eq:dbstar} in $\lambda$,
\[
  A_h(\lambda)\equiv\frac{\mathrm{d}d_h^\ast}{\mathrm{d}\lambda}
    =\frac{S_h\bar\omega}{2\gamma_h}\bigl(1+m/\gamma_h\bigr)^{-3/2},
  \qquad
  A_f(\lambda)\equiv\frac{\mathrm{d}d_f^\ast}{\mathrm{d}\lambda}
    =\frac{\bar D_f}{2\gamma_f}\bigl(1+m/\gamma_f\bigr)^{-3/2},
  \qquad
  \frac{\mathrm{d}d_b^\ast}{\mathrm{d}\lambda}=\frac{1}{\psi}.
\]
Because $D'(\lambda)=A_h+A_f+1/\psi>0$, the change of variables
$\lambda\mapsto D(\lambda)$ is a strictly increasing
$C^{1}$-diffeomorphism, with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dhdD-app}
  \frac{\mathrm{d}d_h^\ast}{\mathrm{d}D}
  \;=\;\frac{1}{\,1+A_f/A_h+1/(\psi A_h)\,}.
\end{equation}
Under the symmetric calibration $\bar r_h=\bar r_f$ and
$\gamma_h=\gamma_f\equiv\gamma$, the $\lambda$-dependent factors in
$A_h$ and $A_f$ are identical, so $A_f/A_h=\bar D_f/(S_h\bar\omega)$
is constant in $\lambda$. The denominator of \eqref{eq:dhdD-app} can
therefore be written
\[
  \varphi(\lambda)
  \;=\;\Bigl(1+\frac{\bar D_f}{S_h\bar\omega}\Bigr)
  \;+\;\frac{2\gamma}{\psi\,S_h\bar\omega}\,(1+m/\gamma)^{3/2},
\]
which is strictly increasing in $\lambda$ on $[\bar r_h,\infty)$, so
$\mathrm{d}d_h^\ast/\mathrm{d}D=1/\varphi(\lambda)$ is strictly
decreasing in $\lambda$. Since $D(\lambda)$ is strictly increasing,
$\mathrm{d}d_h^\ast/\mathrm{d}D$ is also strictly decreasing in $D$,
which is equivalent to strict concavity of $d_h^\ast(D)$. Replacing
the roles of $A_h$ and $A_f$ in the same argument delivers strict
concavity of $d_f^\ast(D)$. $\square$

\section{Robustness of the two-stage estimator}
\whenintegrated{\label{app:robustness}}

The Stage~1 / Stage~2 design recovers a country-level structural-size
effect $\hat\delta_{ij}$ from a fixed-effects regression of sector
size on debt (Equation~\eqref{eq:stage1}), and uses
$\hat\delta_{ij}$ as a country-constant regressor in Stage~2
(Equation~\eqref{eq:stage2}). Two features of this design merit
robustness checks.

\paragraph{Sample mismatch.}
The Stage~1 sample uses GFDD sector-size data, which is sparser than
the Arslanalp--Tsuda holdings data used in Stage~2. After Stage~1,
$\hat\delta_{ij}$ is constructed as the country-level fixed effect
and is propagated to every country-year of country $i$, including
country-years where the underlying size variable was \emph{not}
observed. As a result, Stage~2 includes country-years that were not
in the Stage~1 sample.

\paragraph{Sparsely-measured countries.}
$\hat\delta_{ij}$ is more precisely estimated for countries with many
Stage~1 observations and noisier for countries with few. The
per-country Stage~1 observation counts are: most countries contribute
$\geq\!11$ non-bank size observations; the exceptions are
\textit{Cyprus} ($0$, dropped automatically because $\hat\delta_{i,h}$
is missing), \textit{Luxembourg} ($1$) and \textit{Malta} ($4$).
For the central-bank size, only \textit{Latvia} has fewer than $10$
observations ($9$). For private-bank size, only \textit{Canada}
has fewer than $10$ ($8$).

We address both concerns with two robustness specifications.

\begin{itemize}
  \item[\textbf{(2) Matched sample.}]
        Stage~2 is restricted to country-years that were also in the
        Stage~1 sample for the corresponding sector, eliminating the
        propagated rows (only rows where $s_{ij,it}$ was directly
        observed are kept).
  \item[\textbf{(3) Sparse-country drop.}]
        Stage~2 drops countries with fewer than $k$ Stage~1
        observations for the corresponding sector. We report
        $k\geq\!5$ and $k\geq\!10$.
\end{itemize}

All specifications use the Hansen threshold estimate
$\hat{\bar D}=102{.}7\%$ of GDP (Section~\ref{sec:hansen-threshold}
and Table~\ref{tab:threshold}) and two-way cluster-robust standard errors at
country and year. Robustness regressions are implemented jointly with the
canonical Stage~2 in
\texttt{Code/empirical\_debt\_composition/python/twoway\_cluster\_full.py}; the Stata analogue is
\texttt{Code/empirical\_debt\_composition/do/twoway\_cluster\_full.do}
(driven by \texttt{run\_all.do}).

\paragraph{Summary.}
Two implementation concerns about the two-stage estimator do not materially affect the headline findings. (i) The \textit{propagation} of $\hat\delta_{ij}$ to country-years not in the Stage~1 sample is innocuous: restricting Stage~2 to the matched sample leaves the size-scaling and threshold-slope coefficients within sampling variability of the headline estimates and, for non-banks, slightly strengthens $\theta_h$. (ii) The presence of countries with very few Stage~1 observations (Luxembourg, Malta for non-banks; Latvia, Canada for banking-size variables) does not drive the size-scaling result: dropping these countries leaves the headline coefficients essentially unchanged. The banking-takeover sum $\mu_{cb}+\mu_{pb}$ is between $0.78$ and $0.82$ in every specification.
