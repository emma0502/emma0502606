  This paper develops a three-sector cost-minimization model in which the government allocates public debt across domestic non-banks, foreign investors, and the banking sector. Each sector has a distinct absorption mechanism: non-bank demand is anchored by a country-specific structural size, foreign absorption is bounded by a uniform no-default ceiling, and banks serve as the residual absorber at a convex welfare cost.

We take the model to panel data on advanced economies using a two-stage panel regression that first recovers structural sector sizes and then estimates the debt-allocation rule. The estimates discipline the model's cross-country heterogeneity and yield a tractable framework for interpreting variation in who holds government debt.
